About the Institute

A quarter century in the mountains

Founded in 2001 by the Ridgeline Education Foundation, the institute has grown from a single academic hall and 240 students into a residential campus of 3,400.

Our story

Built one department at a time

The institute opened in August 2001 with three engineering majors taught out of a single academic hall on NC-191. The founding board set one condition that still governs every decision here: no program launches until its laboratories are built and its faculty hired.

That discipline shaped a slow, deliberate expansion — computing in 2004, business in 2007, arts and sciences in 2012 — and a campus that added its buildings only as its departments earned them. Today the institute offers ten degree programs across four schools, holds regional accreditation from SACSCOC, and counts more than 11,000 alumni working across the country.

Asheville gives the campus its character. The Blue Ridge Parkway runs minutes from the main gate, winters bring the annual bonfire on the quad, and the city's growing technology and manufacturing corridor gives students a working economy to learn from.

Vision

What we are building toward

To be a teaching institution of the first rank in the Southeast — one where rigorous fundamentals, honest assessment, and close mentorship produce graduates who are employable on day one and curious for life.

Mission

How we work

Keep classes small and laboratories current; review curricula with industry every two years; keep education affordable through merit and need-based aid; and hold every student to the discipline of attendance, practice, and review.

Milestones

Twenty-five years, briefly

  1. 2001

    Institute founded

    First academic year begins with 240 students in three engineering majors: computer, electrical, and mechanical.

  2. 2004

    School of Computing

    Computer science and information technology programs launch; the first computing laboratory wing is commissioned.

  3. 2007

    School of Business

    The B.S. in Business Administration and the MBA begin, with the institute's first industry advisory board.

  4. 2012

    Arts & Sciences and a new library

    Science and mathematics degrees are introduced; Overlook Library opens with a 400-seat reading room.

  5. 2019

    Engineering Hall & ABET reaccreditation

    All three engineering programs earn ABET reaccreditation as the new Engineering Hall opens.

  6. 2023

    Center for AI & Data Science

    A dedicated center with GPU laboratories opens; the B.S. in Data Science admits its first class.

  7. 2026

    25th anniversary

    The institute marks twenty-five years with an alumni endowment funding fifty full-tuition scholarships.
